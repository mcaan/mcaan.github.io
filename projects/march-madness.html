March Madness Predictions

Building matchup probabilities and tournament brackets for the 2026 NCAA men’s and women’s tournaments—and testing how the best model changes with the scoring objective.

NCAA March Madness 2026Python · PostgreSQL · scikit-learn · XGBoostFeature selection · Ensembles

The question

Can historical game and team statistics produce useful probabilities for unfamiliar tournament matchups? Which approach works best when the goal is accurate probabilities versus a high-scoring bracket?

Both men’s and women’s games are represented as team-pair matchups, with feature differences describing the teams before the model selects a winner.

The approach

01

Prepare historical NCAA results, team statistics, and conference rankings, then construct matchup-level features for modeling.

02

Compare an all-variables logistic regression, a reduced RFECV/pruned logistic model, and XGBoost. Combine the latter two probability estimates in a fixed 50/50 ensemble.

03

Evaluate historical holdout performance, then compare 2026 tournament submissions using Kaggle’s probability-based Brier score and CBS’s round-weighted bracket points.

What the models found

On historical holdout games, the full and reduced logistic models were nearly tied: 77.4% versus 77.2% accuracy, both with 0.466 log loss and 0.858 ROC AUC. The reduced model used substantially fewer features. XGBoost reached 76.1% accuracy and 0.485 log loss.

For the actual 2026 tournament, the winning model depended on the task. The ensemble had the lowest Kaggle Brier score among these models, while the reduced logistic model earned the most CBS bracket points for both tournaments.

What changed the decision

Averaging model probabilities helped the ensemble limit some extreme forecasts and produced the strongest probability score, 0.1483. A bracket discards much of that probability information: once a winner is chosen, the points depend on who advances. There, the reduced logistic model scored 92 men’s points and 123 women’s points, ahead of the other submitted approaches.

The full and reduced logistic models looked almost identical on historical holdout tests, yet their 2026 Brier scores separated sharply (0.2251 versus 0.1530). That contrast supports the project’s attention to redundant features without claiming one tournament proves a general performance advantage.

Scope & limitations

The tournament is a single out-of-sample event, so bracket points can be volatile. The Kaggle and CBS metrics answer different questions and should not be compared as though they shared a scale.

A separate PostgreSQL pipeline reproduces the preparation and matchup engineering for future work. The results above come from the original Python notebooks; they have not been regenerated from the SQL views. The SQL training view requires time-aware feature construction before use in a leakage-free evaluation.

Full methodology, notebooks, limitations, and reproduction instructions are in the repository.
